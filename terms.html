3. Exercise safely

Physical activity carries risk. Start gently, know your limits, and stop if you feel pain, dizziness or shortness of breath.

Suggested routes come from OpenStreetMap data, which is created by volunteers and can be wrong or out of date. A suggested path may be closed, private, unlit or unsafe. Always follow traffic rules and signs, pay attention to your surroundings, and do not look at your phone or watch when it is not safe to.

4. Food, nutrition and allergy information

  • Nutrition figures are estimates. NoNor calculates them from public food tables, product labels, recipe amounts and typical portion weights. Real food varies, and the numbers can be wrong.
  • Allergy warnings are a help, not a guarantee. NoNor looks for allergens in ingredient names and the information it has about foods. It can miss an allergen, it does not know about traces or cross-contamination, and no warning does not mean a food is safe for you. If you or someone you cook for has an allergy or intolerance, always read the product label and check every ingredient yourself.
  • Product information from barcodes comes from Open Food Facts, a database built by volunteers. It can be incomplete or wrong.
  • Imported recipes can be misread. When NoNor reads a recipe from a link, a photo or a document, including with AI, it can get an ingredient, an amount or a step wrong. Check what it imported before you rely on it — allergens especially.
